# Purpose

Translate a product problem into testable requirements and a decision-ready plan without inventing customer evidence.

## Inputs and prerequisites

Identify target users, problem context, business objective, constraints, current workflow, available research, stakeholders, and decision owner.

## Procedure

1. Write the problem before proposing a feature.
2. Separate observed evidence, stakeholder statements, assumptions, and hypotheses.
3. Identify target segment, user need, current workaround, and consequence of the problem.
4. Define desired outcomes and measurable success metrics with numerator, denominator, population, time window, and guardrails.
5. Explore the smallest set of viable solution options and meaningful non-goals.
6. Prioritize using explicit criteria and state trade-offs.
7. Write requirements as observable behavior, not implementation guesses.
8. Add acceptance criteria, edge cases, dependencies, instrumentation, rollout, and open questions.
9. Identify what must be validated through user research or experiment before commitment.

## Evidence requirements

Use actual interviews, analytics, support tickets, experiments, and market evidence. Never fabricate user quotes, research sample sizes, demand, or stakeholder approval.

## Output contract

Produce problem statement, evidence, assumptions, goals/non-goals, requirements, metrics, acceptance criteria, dependencies, risks, and open questions.

## Failure modes and abstention

Do not claim product-market fit, user demand, or causal impact from anecdotal evidence. If user segment or problem is unclear, ask one focused question or state the assumption.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Problem is distinct from proposed solution.
- [ ] Evidence and assumptions are separated.
- [ ] Metrics are operationally defined.
- [ ] Requirements are testable.
- [ ] Non-goals and risks are explicit.
